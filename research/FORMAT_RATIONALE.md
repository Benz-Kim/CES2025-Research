# Research format and benchmarks

CES uses a sequence of thesis notes: mechanism → demand → company diligence → falsification. The structure is intended to make an argument inspectable rather than to collect company profiles.

## Open research software references

[Open Quantum Safe liboqs](https://github.com/open-quantum-safe/liboqs) separates overview, status, limitations, quickstart and documentation. The relevant editorial lesson is that supported capabilities and limitations belong near the entrance to a technical project. This repository adapts that principle with a clear research status and explicit evidence boundaries; it does not claim to implement liboqs.

[The Kyber reference repository](https://github.com/pq-crystals/kyber) exposes reference and optimized implementations and visible testing artifacts. The applicable lesson is to separate a specification from the artifact that can be examined. Here, source records and analytical claims remain separate, and proposed experiments are distinguished from results.

## Institutional research reference

[BIS Paper 158](https://www.bis.org/publications/paper-158-quantum-readiness-financial-system-roadmap) is a useful model for moving from a threat to a migration framework. This repository adapts the question sequence, not its prose or institutional authority.

## What the format enables

A reader can disagree at a specific step: reject the threat assumption, challenge the buyer hypothesis, request a missing company artifact, or propose a test. Each disagreement has an owning document and an evidence requirement. A later update must revise the argument, not merely append another link.
