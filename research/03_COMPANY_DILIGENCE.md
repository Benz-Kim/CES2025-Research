# 03 — Two cases, different evidence burdens

The presentation selects QuSecure and Quranium (D01). They are research cases, not a ranked universe or an endorsement. Sources are in [the register](../sources/REGISTER.md).

## QuSecure: test the orchestration proposition

**Observed evidence:** the public 2024 Frost & Sullivan assessment describes QuProtect's orchestration approach (S10). The company's announcement reports the award (S11). This establishes an identifiable product proposition and analyst recognition.

**Project interpretation:** if centralized cryptographic policy reduces migration work across heterogeneous infrastructure, it may solve an operational problem beyond algorithm implementation. The competing explanation is that native platform upgrades remove enough complexity that an extra layer has limited value.

| Question | Why it changes the conclusion | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which paths and endpoints are protected? | A protected segment is not end-to-end coverage | Architecture and explicit exclusions |
| Which implementation and versions are deployed? | Standards terminology can hide implementation differences | Versioned algorithm inventory and validation scope |
| What happens when negotiation fails? | Availability and downgrade risks interact | Negative tests, logs and rollback design |
| Is the pilot representative? | A demonstration may omit production constraints | Workload, topology, failures and operating duration |
| What is independently verified? | Awards and vendor reports have different evidential weight | Customer-confirmed scope or third-party test artifact |

The slide's deployment and standardization-participation statements remain unresolved in this review. The retrieved award documents do not establish the scope of every claimed government deployment or that a company authors a NIST standard. No current product feature is retroactively assigned to CES 2025.

## Quranium: separate category design from security evidence

**Observed evidence:** the live DeQUIP page presents a company-defined category connecting decentralization and quantum security (S12). Its branding includes absolute security language. That terminology is a vendor claim, not a security conclusion adopted here.

**Project interpretation:** a blockchain-oriented system must explain which signature, network, wallet and operational trust boundaries it protects. A post-quantum component does not demonstrate ledger-wide resilience, decentralization or scalability.

The original deck's Layer-2 characterization and ExeQuantum partnership claim (D01) are not independently established here. ExeQuantum's own documentation describes a PQC service (S13); this does not by itself prove a relationship with Quranium or any specific historical architecture.

| Claim area | Required artifact | Present conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantum resistance | Versioned primitive choices, threat model and security analysis | Undetermined at system level |
| Scalability | Reproducible throughput and latency under specified node/network conditions | Unverified |
| Decentralization | Validator admission, control distribution and upgrade authority | Unverified |
| Wallet security | Key custody, recovery and transaction-signing lifecycle | Unverified |
| Integration or partnership | Joint dated announcement plus technical integration scope | Not established by this source set |

## Comparison result

QuSecure's retrieved public analyst document permits a bounded examination of a migration product. Quranium's retrieved materials permit an examination of positioning and missing assurance artifacts. This asymmetry concerns available evidence, not a measured difference in security, revenue or engineering quality.

Do not calculate a composite vendor score from these unequal source sets. Record missing evidence and decide what observation would change the assessment.
